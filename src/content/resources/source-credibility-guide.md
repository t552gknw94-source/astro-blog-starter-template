CLEAR is a classroom routine: Creator, Lens, Evidence, Alignment, and a Reliability check. Credibility is not a sticker a website wears. It is a match between a source and the job you are asking it to do.

## C — Creator

- Who wrote or produced this?
- What is their position to know? (researcher, reporter, advocate, official, anonymous account)
- Who pays for the work, if you can tell?

If you cannot name a creator, do not build a claim on the source. Find the same information somewhere a person is accountable.

## L — Lens

- What is this piece for: to study, report, persuade, sell, entertain, or organize?
- Which perspective does it center?
- Which words tell you that, besides the headline?

A strong advocate can still be useful. You have to label the lens instead of pretending the piece is neutral.

## E — Evidence

- What is the central claim?
- What is that claim resting on: data, anecdote, authority, a document you can open?
- Can you reach the underlying evidence, or only a summary of it?

## A — Alignment

- Does this source answer your question, or a nearby question you wish you had asked?
- What is it not allowed to prove?

A study of one city cannot quietly become a fact about the country. Write the boundary in the margin.

## R — Reliability check

Open a second source that does not share an owner, a press release, or a citation trail with the first. Then finish this sentence:

“After reading both, I am more / less confident that …, because ….”

## A note you can paste above a works-cited entry

Creator and role. Lens. What I am using it to show. What I am not using it to show.

If you cannot write that note, you are not ready to cite the source.
