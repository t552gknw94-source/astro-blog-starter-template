Use this after a draft exists. It is a companion to College Board’s official rubrics, not a replacement. If a box and the current AP Seminar rubric ever seem to disagree, follow the official rubric and ask your teacher.

Confirm word count, citation format, and task rules in the current course materials before you submit.

## The claim

- [ ] A reader can find the central claim without guessing.
- [ ] The claim is debatable. A reasonable person could argue a different conclusion.
- [ ] The question is still visible inside the claim. The paper did not drift to an easier topic.

## The line

- [ ] Reasons appear in an order. Each one prepares the next.
- [ ] I can say the line of reasoning out loud in four sentences or fewer.
- [ ] No section exists only because I found an interesting source.

## Evidence and commentary

- [ ] Every major reason has evidence, and the evidence is attributed.
- [ ] Commentary explains how the evidence supports the reason. It does more than repeat the quotation.
- [ ] I have marked at least one place where a source complicates my claim, and I respond to it.
- [ ] Paraphrase is in my syntax. I did not borrow the source’s sentence shape.

## The close and the defense

- [ ] The conclusion answers “so what” for the audience. It does not paste the introduction.
- [ ] I can name a limitation of my own argument.
- [ ] I can explain, without notes, why I trusted one source more than another.

## A ten-minute pass

Read only the first sentence of each paragraph. If those sentences do not form the argument, the commentary is hiding the line. Repair the topic sentences before you polish the quotations.
