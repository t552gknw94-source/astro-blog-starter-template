Build one paragraph. Then try a second without looking at the boxes. The organizer is a temporary scaffold.

## The boxes

**Point.** One sentence. The job of this paragraph in the larger argument.

**Evidence.** A quotation, paraphrase, or data point a reader could check. Name the source.

**Commentary, sentence one.** What the evidence is doing. Explain it. Do not add a new fact yet.

**Commentary, sentence two.** How this evidence supports the point, and what it lets you say next.

**Limit, if you need one.** What this evidence does not prove.

## A worked example

**Point.** The group’s repeated “we” sounds like unity, but the details that follow show the group leaving people out.

**Evidence.** In the opening, the narrator says “we knew who belonged on the porch,” and then lists three families by name.

**Commentary.** The sentence offers a shared knowing, then immediately narrows it to a list. Belonging is treated as something the speaker can inventory.

**Commentary.** That move supports the claim that the community’s unity is performed. The reader is handed a boundary at the same moment they are handed the word “we.”

**Limit.** One sentence cannot prove the narrator is unreliable. It can show that membership is being policed.

## A check before you move on

- Cover the evidence. Does the point still make a claim, or did the quotation do all the work?
- Cover the point. Does the commentary talk about the evidence, or about a different idea?
- Read the paragraph aloud. Mark any sentence you could not explain.

When the shape is in your ear, retire the boxes. A summative draft should sound like you, not like a form.
