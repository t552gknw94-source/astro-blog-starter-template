This protocol is the classroom rule for research writing in 11th Grade English and AP Seminar. A tool may help you find, question, and revise. It may not own the claim. If your teacher’s directions for a specific task are stricter than this page, follow those directions.

## Phase 1 — Think first

Complete this before you open a generative tool.

- Question, with scope:
- Evidence I think I need:
- Two doubts I already have:
- Keywords I can try on my own:

If you cannot fill this, you are not ready for a tool. You are ready for more reading or a conference.

## Phase 2 — Give the tool a narrow job

Allowed, if you disclose it in your process note:

- suggesting additional search terms
- listing perspectives you should go read for yourself
- explaining a confusing sentence from a source, which you then check against the source
- pointing at a paragraph of yours that is hard to follow

Ask first:

- feedback on a thesis you wrote
- help noticing missing counterarguments
- an outline built from notes you wrote

Not allowed:

- a thesis, paragraph, or presentation script you did not write
- fabricated sources, or quotations you have not found in the original
- pasting another student’s work, a peer’s story, or anything private
- asking a tool to mimic a specific person’s voice or to hide how a draft was made

## Phase 3 — Verify

For anything you keep, retrieve a real source and read the relevant part.

| What the tool offered | Source I opened | Fair / unfair to the source | What I kept, in my own words |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |  |

## Phase 4 — Write the reasoning yourself

Your process note, attached to the draft or written at the end:

1. What did I ask the tool to do?
2. What did I reject?
3. Which source confirmed the part I kept?
4. Which sentences can I explain out loud without looking?

Read the draft aloud. Mark any sentence you cannot defend. Rebuild those sentences before you submit. The thinking in the paper has to be thinking you can stand next to in the room.
