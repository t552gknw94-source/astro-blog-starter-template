Use this when a claim shows up in a caption, a video, a headline, or a conversation. The whole routine can fit in one class period. The habit is the point.

## 1. Pause before you share

Do not repost, argue, or drop the claim into a paper yet. Write the time. Urgency is part of how weak claims travel.

## 2. Name the claim in one sentence

Put it in your own words. Include the number, the place, or the group if the original had one. If the original is too vague to restate (“everything is rigged”), say that. Vagueness is a finding.

## 3. Open a second source

Choose a source with a different method or owner: a primary document, a newsroom that links to its evidence, or a research summary that shows where the numbers came from. Do not open a second post that is quoting the first one. That is the same source wearing a different font.

- Source I started with:
- Second source:
- What the second source confirms, narrows, or contradicts:

## 4. Record the change in your confidence

Finish both lines, even if the change is small.

- Before the second source, I would have said:
- Now I would say:
- I would / would not repeat the original wording, because:

## For the teacher

Model the routine on a claim you do not already know the answer to. Students learn more from watching a confident adult update a belief than from watching a performance of certainty. Keep a shared list of “claims we retired” and “claims we narrowed.” Both lists count as learning.
